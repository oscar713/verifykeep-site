Bank Feed Expired? Getting Historical Transactions Out of PDFs

November 14, 2026 · VerifyKeep

Bank feeds are the easy path — until they aren't. A new client hands you three years of PDFs for a catch-up job, the account switched banks, the feed only reaches back 90 days, or the institution was never supported in the first place. The transactions exist. They're just trapped inside PDFs.

Where the feed runs out

QuickBooks Online and Xero feeds are limited in ways that only matter when it's already a problem:

  • Lookback limits — most banks serve 90 days; some stretch to 12–24 months. Older history doesn't exist as far as the feed is concerned.
  • Dropped connections — feeds silently disconnect, and missing stretches never backfill.
  • Closed accounts — once an account closes, its feed dies with it.
  • Unsupported institutions — credit unions and smaller banks often never connect at all.

For any of those, the only authoritative source is the statement itself — the PDF the bank produced.

Why "just convert them" fails at scale

Converting one statement is easy. Converting 36 statements is where sloppy tools cost you. Multiply the standard failure modes — wrapped descriptions becoming phantom rows, swapped debit/credit columns, dropped transactions at page breaks — across three years, and small per-statement errors compound into a books problem nobody can reconcile.

The volume changes the requirement: you don't need rows that look right, you need rows that are provably right, statement by statement.

The batch workflow that works

  1. Collect every statement — checking, savings, credit cards, and any combined multi-account documents.
  2. Upload in batch — process the whole set at once, not one painful file at a time.
  3. Validate each statement — every converted file reconciles to its own printed totals: transaction count, credits, debits, closing balance. A statement that doesn't balance is flagged, not shipped.
  4. Review only the exceptions — flagged rows open side-by-side against the original page; clean statements need no eyeball time.
  5. Export in your software's format — CSV, QuickBooks, or Xero — and import.

Step 3 is what makes batch conversion safe: an arithmetic proof per statement, not a leap of faith across three years.

Month-end and catch-up jobs

This is the month-end pattern: statements land all at once and the clock is running. The right tooling treats batch as the default — upload everything, validate everything, touch only what's flagged. If a page fails to process, it's refunded automatically; blank pages are free; you see the page count and cost before anything runs.
