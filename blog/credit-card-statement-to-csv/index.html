Convert a Credit Card Statement to CSV Without Getting the Signs Wrong

November 15, 2026 · VerifyKeep

Credit card statements are the statements most often converted wrong — not because the extraction is harder, but because the sign convention is backwards. On a bank account, money in is a credit. On a card statement, a "payment" reduces what you owe and a "purchase" increases it. A converter built for checking accounts gets the direction wrong on every single row, and the file looks perfectly fine in Excel until reconciliation day.

Where generic converters break on card statements

  • Flipped signs — purchases land as negative, payments as positive; the totals still sum, just backwards.
  • Payments mixed into charges — "Payment — Thank You" rows and purchases share the same layout on most issuers' PDFs.
  • Interest and fee lines — printed in summary sections, dropped entirely or duplicated as transactions.
  • Rewards summaries — points balances get extracted as phantom dollar rows.
  • Scans — photographed card statements need OCR, and OCR misreads decimals on dense transaction tables.

The check: reconcile to the printed totals

The card statement prints its own answer key: previous balance, payments/credits, purchases, fees, interest, and new balance. If the extracted rows can't reproduce new balance from the rest, something is wrong — and you can see exactly which bucket is off.

Verify before the CSV goes anywhere:

  1. Transaction count matches the printed count (charges + payments + fees).
  2. Previous balance − payments + purchases + fees + interest = printed new balance.
  3. Signs verified against a known row — a real purchase must land positive.

The VerifyKeep workflow

  1. Upload the card statement PDF — scans route to OCR automatically.
  2. Page count and cost shown before processing; blank pages are free.
  3. Extraction applies the correct sign convention for card statements, then reconciles every figure against the statement's printed totals — balance, counts, deposit/withdrawal buckets, completeness, anomalies.
  4. Flagged rows open side-by-side against the original page for review.
  5. Export a clean CSV — date, description, signed amount — ready for Excel, QuickBooks, or Xero.

If the statement doesn't reconcile, export is blocked: the report shows which bucket failed and by how much. Failed pages are refunded automatically.

Importing a card statement

For QuickBooks or Xero, the card account imports like a bank account — date, payee, signed amount. Because the signs were verified before export, the charges land as expenses and the payment lands as a transfer, which is what the reconciliation expects.
