Convert a Bank Statement PDF to Xero (Verified Before Import)

November 14, 2026 · VerifyKeep

Xero's bank reconciliation is clean when a live feed works. When it doesn't — an unsupported bank, a dropped connection, or months of history the feed can't reach — the transactions are locked inside a PDF and someone has to get them out.

Here's how to do that without re-typing, and the one step that decides whether the result is trustworthy.

When the feed can't help

Xero's direct bank feeds cover most major banks, but they have gaps: smaller credit unions, accounts that were closed before the feed was connected, and history that only reaches back so far. Catch-up work — a new client mid-year, a late reconciliation, an audit — almost always means PDFs.

What Xero needs

Xero imports bank statements as CSV or OFX with three essentials: a date, a payee/description, and a signed amount (money in positive, money out negative). Get the sign convention wrong and every row imports backwards.

The traps in a generic PDF-to-CSV conversion:

  • Credit-card direction — a card statement's "payments" and "purchases" need opposite signs to a bank account's.
  • Wrapped descriptions — a two-line merchant name becomes a phantom second row with no amount, or worse, a phantom transaction.
  • Dropped or duplicated rows — silently shifts the running balance.
  • Scans — photographed statements need OCR first, and OCR guesses at decimals.

The check: does it reconcile to the printed balance?

The statement itself contains the answer key: opening balance, total deposits, total withdrawals, closing balance. If the extracted rows don't reproduce those numbers exactly, the conversion failed somewhere — a missing transaction, a flipped sign, a misread decimal.

Before anything reaches Xero, verify:

  1. Extracted transaction count matches the statement's printed count
  2. Opening balance + credits − debits = the printed closing balance
  3. Flagged or low-confidence rows are checked against the original page

The VerifyKeep workflow

  1. Upload the PDF — checking, savings, credit card, brokerage or combined statements; scanned pages are routed to OCR automatically.
  2. See the page count and cost before processing (blank pages are free).
  3. Every transaction is extracted and checked against the bank's own printed figures — balance reconciliation, counts, totals, completeness, anomalies.
  4. Flagged rows open in a side-by-side review next to the original PDF.
  5. Export a Xero-ready CSV (date, description, signed amount) and import it.

If the statement doesn't balance, the export is blocked — the report shows which check failed and by how much. Failed pages are refunded automatically.

Importing into Xero

In the bank account's Manage menu → Import a statement → choose the CSV → map the columns to Xero's Date / Payee / Amount fields → review and reconcile. Because the rows already reconcile to the statement, the For Review tab fills cleanly instead of surfacing mismatches.
