Why Your Converted Bank Statement Doesn't Balance (and How to Check)

November 14, 2026 · VerifyKeep

You ran a bank statement through a converter, imported the file, and reconciliation exploded. The closing balance in your spreadsheet is $14.20 off — or $1,400 off. This is the most common outcome of naive conversion, and it's almost always one of four causes.

The math every statement gives you

Every bank statement prints its own answer key: opening balance, total credits (deposits), total debits (withdrawals), and closing balance. The identity is simple:

opening balance + credits − debits = closing balance

If your converted file doesn't satisfy that identity, the conversion failed somewhere — no exceptions. The failure is almost always invisible until you check.

Failure mode 1: Wrapped descriptions become phantom rows

Most statements wrap long transaction descriptions onto a second line. A naive parser treats that second line as a new transaction — usually with no amount, or with an amount pulled from the wrong column. You get an extra row, a wrong count, and a broken total.

Check: count your extracted transactions against the statement's printed transaction count. Off by a few? Look for empty or mislabeled rows.

Failure mode 2: Debit and credit columns swapped

Banks label columns differently — "Withdrawal" vs "Debit" vs "Amount (−)". A converter that guesses column direction drops every transaction into the wrong side. The extracted file looks fine until the balance check fails by exactly twice the swapped amount.

Check: reconcile opening + credits − debits against the printed closing. A difference of exactly 2× a transaction amount means a sign flip on that row.

Failure mode 3: Credit-card statements read like checking

Credit card statements reconcile backwards — the "balance" is debt owed, not cash on hand. Purchases increase the balance; payments decrease it. A converter that treats a card statement like a checking account gets the sign wrong on every single row, and the totals still "add up" in a misleading way.

Check: compare payment transactions specifically — they should reduce the balance, not increase it.

Failure mode 4: Dropped transactions

One missed row and the file is wrong — silently. Dropped transactions happen at page boundaries, around wrapped rows, and on scanned pages where OCR confidence was low and the tool didn't tell you.

Check: extracted count vs printed count, then opening + credits − debits vs closing. Both must match.

The 60-second check

Before you import any converted statement, verify three numbers against the printed summary:

  1. Transaction count — extracted rows = printed count
  2. Balance identity — opening + credits − debits = closing
  3. Deposit and withdrawal totals — each matches its printed total

If your converter doesn't perform this check, you're performing it by hand — every single time.
