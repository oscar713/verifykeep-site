Export Brokerage Statement Transactions From PDF to a Verified CSV

November 15, 2026 · VerifyKeep

Brokerage statements are the hardest PDFs in the category — and the ones no converter bothers to check. Schwab, Fidelity, Merrill and the rest print multi-section statements: cash management activity, dividend and interest tables, trade confirms, fee schedules, all interleaved with holdings summaries that look like transactions but aren't.

When a client hands you one of these for catch-up work, here's how to get the transactions out — and prove they're right.

Why brokerage PDFs defeat generic converters

  • Holdings tables aren't transactions — positions printed with quantities and values get extracted as phantom cash movements.
  • Multiple cash sections — a combined statement can carry a core cash account plus a margin or sweep section; rows land in the wrong account.
  • Share quantities in the amount column — a buy prints shares and dollars; naive extraction picks the wrong column.
  • Reinvestments — dividend reinvestments print twice: once as income, once as a purchase. Some layouts mean count them once, others twice.
  • Scans — older or mailed statements need OCR, which misreads the dense multi-column trade tables.

The check: does the cash section reconcile?

Every brokerage statement prints the cash movement totals — beginning cash balance, deposits, income, disbursements, trades settled, ending cash balance. That's the answer key for the transactions section: if extracted cash rows don't reproduce the printed ending balance, something was dropped, doubled, or pulled from a holdings table.

  1. Cash-section transaction count matches the printed activity count.
  2. Beginning cash + deposits + income − disbursements − settled trades = printed ending cash balance.
  3. Flagged rows (dense tables, OCR uncertainty) reviewed against the original page.

The VerifyKeep workflow

  1. Upload the statement — checking, savings, credit card, brokerage, or combined multi-account documents; scans route to OCR automatically.
  2. Page count and cost shown before processing; blank pages are free.
  3. Extraction separates transaction sections from holdings, then reconciles against the broker's printed totals — balance, counts, in/out totals, completeness, anomalies.
  4. Flagged rows open side-by-side against the original PDF page.
  5. Export a verified CSV — date, description, signed amount — for Excel, QuickBooks, or Xero.

Statements that don't reconcile are blocked from export: the report names the failed check and the variance. Failed pages are refunded automatically.

A note on what's in the CSV

The export carries cash movements — deposits, withdrawals, dividends, interest, fees, and trade settlements as dated rows. Holdings snapshots and unrealized gains are summaries, not transactions, and stay out of the file.
