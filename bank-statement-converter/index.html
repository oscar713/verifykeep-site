Why verification matters more than conversion

A converted file that doesn't balance is worse than no file — you import it, post it, and find the discrepancy at month-end. VerifyKeep runs five checks against the statement's printed figures and shows the arithmetic for each one:

  • Balance proof — opening + deposits − withdrawals = printed closing balance, to the cent
  • Transaction count — extracted rows must match the statement
  • Deposit & withdrawal totals — compared against the bank's printed totals
  • Completeness — every page read, every date inside the statement period
  • Anomalies — unusual or low-confidence rows flagged and highlighted for review

If a check fails, the export is blocked and the report tells you which check failed and by how much. No silent fixes — amounts are never adjusted to force a balance.

Scanned statements are the edge case most tools fail

Photos and scans of paper statements need OCR before extraction, and OCR guesses — a "5" becomes an "S", a decimal point disappears. VerifyKeep routes scans through OCR, then applies the same reconciliation: the printed totals are the answer key, and anything that can't be read confidently is flagged next to the original page for side-by-side review instead of slipping into your export.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a bank statement PDF to Excel or CSV?

Upload the PDF — VerifyKeep extracts every transaction, reconciles totals against the printed figures, and lets you download a generic CSV for Excel or a formatted file for QuickBooks Online or Xero.

Can you convert a scanned bank statement?

Yes. Scans are routed through OCR automatically, then validated against the statement's own totals. Rows that can't be read confidently are flagged for review rather than silently included.

Does the converted file match the bank's totals?

It has to — opening balance + deposits − withdrawals must equal the printed closing balance, and transaction counts and totals must match. Export is blocked until it balances.
